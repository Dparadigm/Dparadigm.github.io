1 Thesis

Garrett's edge isn't any one ad. It's a system. One face records one weekly sit-down. That recording gets cut into about 4 clips a day, a weekly live, blog posts, and podcast audio, and his paid ads point to free offers (a free book where you pay shipping, a free digital book, an overview video) that feed email, quizzes, and an application (cadence.md, ads-research.md). He also uses pre-launch mechanics: X1 Wealth ran a "Founding Member" waitlist before opening (whimsical-board.md §8), and Multiplier uses a one-question qualifier before the application (ads-research.md).

We already have most of the raw inputs: Patrick, a 482-episode podcast, a book with a confirmed free-plus-shipping offer (not live yet), the 4-3-2-1 whitepaper, and Family Office 360 in build. The WealthScore is coming but not live yet. That's an opening, not a gap: a waitlist could build an audience that understands the 4-3-2-1 system before the score exists, so launch day has people ready to use it.

Could copy

  • The cadence
  • The repurposing
  • The single face
  • The offer ladder
  • The pre-launch waitlist
  • The one-question qualifier
  • Tracking on organic posts

Shouldn't copy

  • Absolute claims ("safer than banks," "untouchable," guarantees)
  • Scarcity that isn't real ("Founding Member spots are limited" unless it's true)
  • The sameness. Garrett, Wealth Factory, and the Infinite Banking crowd all sell Rockefeller, family bank, and "be your own bank."
Our lane could be measurable architecture: the 4-3-2-1 system, a score, and a ranked Gap Report. "Our product is architecture" (Patrick, Rally, Oct 19) is the line that sets us apart.

3.1 Honesty rules (apply to every option)

  1. Say plainly: "The WealthScore isn't live yet." Every page, ad, clip CTA, and email says it's coming, never that it's available now.
  2. No launch date in public copy until Dan or Mark confirms one. "Coming soon" or "launching later this year" only if leadership approves that wording.
  3. "Founding cohort" or "early access" only describes what's true: people on the list hear first and get access first. No "limited spots" unless spots really are limited.
  4. Keep the score disclaimer from whitepaper p24 and p97 wherever the score is described: it "does not predict markets or guarantee outcomes."
  5. Never promise a score of 100 means "fully on track" (a household meeting every benchmark scores about 84, Appendix A working session).
  6. Don't promote FO360 features that won't be live at release (Rachel's page; whitepaper notes §6).

9 Tools and production

  • Riverside: Magic Clips and captions for first cuts. A human edits and approves every clip. A recent Avalaunch ad looked like an unreviewed Riverside Magic edit, which is the failure to avoid.
  • AI clipping (Riverside, Opus Clip, or Descript) helps with finding moments, captions, reframing, and draft titles. It doesn't help with picking compliant moments, catching absolute claims, or brand tone. Those stay human.
  • AI faceless video: Rachel's page says a faceless AI version of the Core Content Series comes first, for internal feel only. Public content could keep Patrick on camera. Garrett shows no sign of AI avatars (ads-research.md).
  • Metricool: the Paradigm Life brand connects FB, IG, X, LinkedIn, YouTube, and both ad accounts. Posts go out only after approval.
  • YouTube: connect it in Riverside hosting so every episode publishes with video. Riverside platform links currently list only Apple and Spotify.
  • Spotify and Apple video: Spotify needs manual MP4 uploads per episode in Spotify for Creators. Apple needs video approval plus an API key, applies only going forward, and may need the Riverside Grow plan or higher (earlier chat).
  • HubSpot: waitlist form, lead-source label, life-stage and dimension properties, the waitlist workflow, and a utm_content per clip.
