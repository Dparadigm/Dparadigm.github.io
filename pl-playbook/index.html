1 Thesis

Garrett's edge isn't any one ad. It's a system. One face records one weekly sit-down. That recording gets cut into about 4 clips a day, a weekly live, blog posts, and podcast audio, and his paid ads point to free offers (a free book where you pay shipping, a free digital book, an overview video) that feed email, quizzes, and an application (cadence.md, ads-research.md). His high-end offer, Multiplier Family Office, leads to a "Report of Findings" (ads-research.md). He also uses pre-launch mechanics: X1 Wealth ran a "Founding Member" waitlist before opening (whimsical-board.md §8), and Multiplier uses a one-question qualifier before the application (ads-research.md).

We already have most of the raw inputs: Patrick, a 482-episode podcast, a book with a confirmed free-plus-shipping offer (not live yet), the 4-3-2-1 whitepaper, a live WealthScore, and Family Office 360 in build. The score is our quiz and qualifier, and it already works today. Family Office 360 could be the next step after the score, the way Garrett's Report of Findings follows his qualifier. Until FO360 opens, a waitlist captures that next step honestly.

Could copy

  • The cadence
  • The repurposing
  • The single face
  • The offer ladder
  • The score-then-next-step flow
  • The pre-launch waitlist
  • The one-question qualifier
  • Tracking on organic posts

Shouldn't copy

  • Absolute claims ("safer than banks," "untouchable," guarantees)
  • Scarcity that isn't real ("Founding Member spots are limited" unless it's true)
  • The sameness. Garrett, Wealth Factory, and the Infinite Banking crowd all sell Rockefeller, family bank, and "be your own bank."
Our lane could be measurable architecture: the 4-3-2-1 system, a score, and a ranked Gap Report. "Our product is architecture" (Patrick, Rally, Oct 19) is the line that sets us apart.

3.1 What's live today, and how this draft fits around it

The Avalaunch flow (paid, owned by Avalaunch and Kyle): paid ad, then paradigmlife.net/4321 (an Unbounce A/B test called "Villian Page" with three variants), then "Get My Free WealthScore" to portal.paradigmlife.net/wealthscore.

VariantHeadline and angle
a"You Followed All the Traditional Financial Rules. So Why Does Your Financial Future Still Feel So Fragile?" (the architecture problem)
bThe 4-3-2-1 Framework: "We Don't Ask 'Do You Have Enough?'..." plus the pillars, the Hierarchy, and a Family Bank section
cThe Perpetual Wealth Strategy: "Financial Certainty Isn't a Bigger Number..." (purpose and legacy)

All three repeat the WealthScore CTA (variant c also uses "Measure My Certainty") and carry the same three client quotes with a disclosure line. The page itself has no form; every CTA links to the score.

The WealthScore entry page: "First, where should we send your results?" It asks for first name, last name, and email, says "Typically about 3 minutes to complete," saves progress in the browser, and carries a disclaimer that it's an educational self-assessment, not a prediction, guarantee, or personalized advice. What happens after the result (results page, follow-up email, next CTA) is Unverified; we didn't submit the form.

How this draft fits: Avalaunch owns the paid /4321 flow. Everything below is meant to complement it, not compete: organic channels (podcast, clips, blog, newsletters, live, book) send their own traffic to the score with their own tracking, and the FO360 waitlist adds a next step after the score that the paid flow doesn't have yet.

Honesty rules (apply to every option)

  1. WealthScore copy can say it's free and available now. Keep the score's own disclaimer language, and the whitepaper's (p24, p97): it "does not predict markets or guarantee outcomes."
  2. Never promise a score of 100 means "fully on track" (a household meeting every benchmark scores about 84, Appendix A working session).
  3. Family Office 360 copy says plainly that it isn't open yet. No launch date in public copy until Dan or Mark confirms one. "Coming soon" only if leadership approves that wording.
  4. "Founding" or "early access" only describes what's true. No "limited spots" unless spots really are limited.
  5. Don't promote FO360 features that won't be live at release (Rachel's page; whitepaper notes §6). Per Ninety, the Gap Report UI and consultation booking are due Dec 31.

9 Tools and production

  • Riverside: Magic Clips and captions for first cuts. A human edits and approves every clip. A recent Avalaunch ad looked like an unreviewed Riverside Magic edit, which is the failure to avoid.
  • AI clipping (Riverside, Opus Clip, or Descript) helps with finding moments, captions, reframing, and draft titles. It doesn't help with picking compliant moments, catching absolute claims, or brand tone. Those stay human.
  • AI faceless video: Rachel's page says a faceless AI version of the Core Content Series comes first, for internal feel only. Public content could keep Patrick on camera. Garrett shows no sign of AI avatars (ads-research.md).
  • Metricool: the Paradigm Life brand connects FB, IG, X, LinkedIn, YouTube, and both ad accounts. Posts go out only after approval.
  • YouTube: connect it in Riverside hosting so every episode publishes with video. Riverside platform links currently list only Apple and Spotify.
  • Spotify and Apple video: Spotify needs manual MP4 uploads per episode in Spotify for Creators. Apple needs video approval plus an API key, applies only going forward, and may need the Riverside Grow plan or higher (earlier chat).
  • HubSpot: FO360 waitlist form, lead-source label, life-stage and dimension properties, the waitlist workflow, and organic UTMs on every WealthScore link.
